The difference is about **direction** of the signal on GPIO pin 21:

- `GPIO.setup(21, GPIO.IN)`  
  sets GPIO 21 as an **input** pin.  
  The Raspberry Pi **reads** voltage from whatever is connected to it (e.g., a PIR sensor, button, or switch) and you get `0`
  or `1` with `GPIO.input(21)`.
  [raspi](https://raspi.tv/2013/rpi-gpio-basics-4-setting-up-rpi-gpio-numbering-systems-and-inputs)

- `GPIO.setup(21, GPIO.OUT)`  
  sets GPIO 21 as an **output** pin.  
  The Raspberry Pi **drives** a voltage (HIGH/LOW) to whatever is connected (e.g., an LED, a relay, or another circuit) that
  you control with `GPIO.output(21, GPIO.HIGH)` or `GPIO.output(21, GPIO.LOW)`.
  [forums.raspberrypi](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=193890)

### Simple analogy

Think of the pin as a wire between two devices:

- `GPIO.IN` = the Pi **listens** to that wire.
- `GPIO.OUT` = the Pi **talks** on that wire.
  [electronicsforyou](https://electronicsforyou.com/blog/all-about-raspberry-pi-gpio-pins)
