**GND in GPIO stands for ground.** It serves as the 0V reference point on boards like Raspberry Pi or Arduino, completing
circuits for sensors, LEDs, and other components.
[reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/arduino/comments/1l5iszm/why_is_gnd_pin_outputting_signal/)

## Key Role

GND pins provide a common return path for current, ensuring stable voltage measurements relative to other pins (like 3.3V or
5V). Without connecting GND, circuits won't function as there's no complete loop for electricity to flow.
[pcbasic](https://www.pcbasic.com/blog/what_is_gnd.html)

## Usage Tips

- Multiple GND pins exist on GPIO headers (e.g., pins 6, 9, 14, 20, 25 on Raspberry Pi) for convenience.
  [siminnovations](https://siminnovations.com/wiki/index.php/General_Raspberry_Pi_I/O_pin_explanation)
- Always share GND between your board and external devices to avoid floating voltages or damage.
  [forums.raspberrypi](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=80713)
- Logic low on a GPIO pin equals GND (0V), while high is VCC relative to it.
  [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/arduino/comments/1l5iszm/why_is_gnd_pin_outputting_signal/) In projects, connect device
  GND to board GND alongside power and signal lines for reliable operation.
  [kevsrobots](https://www.kevsrobots.com/learn/micropython_gpio/16_ground_pins.html)
