GPIO stands for **General Purpose Input/Output** and refers to the row of small metal pins on the Raspberry Pi that let it
exchange electrical signals with external electronic components.
[luisllamas](https://www.luisllamas.es/en/how-to-use-gpio-raspberry-pi/)

### What GPIO pins do

Each GPIO pin can be configured in software as either:

- **Input** – to read values from sensors, buttons, or other devices (e.g., detecting a button press).
  [luisllamas](https://www.luisllamas.es/en/how-to-use-gpio-raspberry-pi/)
- **Output** – to send signals to control things like LEDs, motors, relays, or displays.
  [w3schools](https://www.w3schools.com/nodejs/nodejs_raspberrypi_gpio_intro.asp)

They are usually **digital** (only two states: on/high or off/low), and most Pi models run them at **3.3 V**, so you must not
connect higher voltages directly or you can damage the board.
[botland.com](https://botland.com.pl/blog/wyprowadzenia-gpio-raspberry-pi-jak-to-dziala/)

### How many pins and what they’re used for

Modern Raspberry Pi boards (like Pi 3/4/5 and Zero‑class models) typically have a **40‑pin GPIO header**, with:

- A few dedicated **power pins** (3.3 V, 5 V) and **ground (GND)** pins.
  [tme](https://www.tme.eu/en/news/library-articles/page/73596/gpio-functions-and-applications-on-the-raspberry-pi/)
- Multiple **general‑purpose GPIOs** that can also be multiplexed for protocols such as **I²C, SPI, and UART** to talk to
  chips, sensors, and other microcontrollers.
  [learn.sparkfun](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/introduction-to-the-raspberry-pi-gpio-and-physical-computing/gpio-pins-overview)

### Practical role in projects

GPIO is what makes Raspberry Pi useful for **physical computing**: it lets you bridge the Pi’s software world (Python, etc.)
with the real‑world hardware like lights, sensors, and actuators.
[luisllamas](https://www.luisllamas.es/en/how-to-use-gpio-raspberry-pi/) Because GPIOs are sensitive, projects usually use
resistors, level‑shifting circuits, or interface boards (such as motor drivers or Pi “hats”) to protect the Pi.
[tme](https://www.tme.eu/en/news/library-articles/page/73596/gpio-functions-and-applications-on-the-raspberry-pi/)

You can connect a wide range of electronic components and modules to the Raspberry Pi’s GPIO, as long as you respect its
**3.3 V logic levels** and do not draw too much current from the pins.
[electronicsforyou](https://electronicsforyou.com/blog/all-about-raspberry-pi-gpio-pins)

### Simple on/off devices

- Small **LEDs** (with a current‑limiting resistor).
  [electronicsforyou](https://electronicsforyou.com/blog/all-about-raspberry-pi-gpio-pins)
- **Push buttons, switches, and sensors** (e.g., motion/PIR, temperature‑humidity sensors like DHT22) used as digital inputs.
  [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskProgramming/comments/1141hql/what_interesting_input_devices_can_i_add_to_a/)
- **Relays and transistors** to let the Pi safely control higher‑voltage or higher‑current loads (for example, lamps or
  motors). [tme](https://www.tme.eu/en/news/library-articles/page/73596/gpio-functions-and-applications-on-the-raspberry-pi/)

### Communication‑based modules

Using the special protocol pins (I²C, SPI, UART) grouped in the GPIO header:

- **I²C devices**: sensors (temperature, color, accelerometer), OLED or small displays, RTC modules.
  [electronicsforyou](https://electronicsforyou.com/blog/all-about-raspberry-pi-gpio-pins)
- **SPI devices**: fast ADCs/DACs, TFT screens, SD cards, RFID readers, some motor drivers.
  [electronicsforyou](https://electronicsforyou.com/blog/all-about-raspberry-pi-gpio-pins)
- **UART serial devices**: GPS modules, some displays, and other microcontrollers (Arduino, ESP‑series when levels are
  compatible).
  [community.element14](https://community.element14.com/products/raspberry-pi/b/blog/posts/essential-raspberry-pi-peripherals-1-gpio-and-other-connectors)

### Other useful things

- **Motor drivers** (like L298N) or H‑bridges to drive DC or stepper motors.
  [electronicsforyou](https://electronicsforyou.com/blog/all-about-raspberry-pi-gpio-pins)
- **Expansion boards (HATs)** and add‑on boards that plug directly into the 40‑pin header, such as motor‑control HATs,
  display HATs, or sensor boards.
  [community.element14](https://community.element14.com/products/raspberry-pi/b/blog/posts/essential-raspberry-pi-peripherals-1-gpio-and-other-connectors)
- Small **keypads, joysticks, sound sensors, and other “fun” inputs** that turn your Pi into a custom controller or
  measurement device.
  [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskProgramming/comments/1141hql/what_interesting_input_devices_can_i_add_to_a/)

As a rule of thumb, GPIO pins are not for plugging in raw mains‑voltage circuits; always use appropriate buffers, drivers, or
breakout boards to keep the Pi safe.
[tme](https://www.tme.eu/en/news/library-articles/page/73596/gpio-functions-and-applications-on-the-raspberry-pi/)
