The confusion comes from the fact that there are three different ways to name the pins on a Raspberry Pi. It’s like a house
having a street address, a plot number, and a name (e.g., "The Smith House"). Here is why Pin 40 is called GPIO21:

1. Physical Numbering (Board) This is simply the count of the metal "legs" on the board, from 1 to 40.

- Pin 40 is the very last physical pin at the corner of the connector.
- This system is easy to find with your eyes, but it doesn't tell you what the pin does.

2. BCM Numbering (Broadcom SoC) This is the "logical" name used by the processor (the Broadcom chip).

- GPIO21 is the name the CPU uses to identify that specific internal switch.
- Most Python libraries (like RPi.GPIO or gpiozero) prefer BCM numbering because it’s consistent across different Raspberry
  Pi models, even if the physical layout changes slightly.

3. Functional Name Some pins have special "jobs" (like I2C, SPI, or UART).

- Pin 40 (GPIO21) is often used as a general-purpose pin, but it can also function as PCM_DOUT (for digital audio).
